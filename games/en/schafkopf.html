Schafkopf

Call an ace or play alone for 61 points · 4 players · Card games

What is it?

Schafkopf is played by four with the Bavarian deck: acorns, leaves, hearts and bells, with Ober, Unter, king and ace (the “Sau”). In a call game all Obers, all Unters and all hearts are trumps, and the declarer gets a partner by calling an ace. In a solo one player takes on the other three alone.

How it’s played

Setup: The game uses the Bavarian deck: 7, 8, 9, 10, Unter, Ober, king and ace (the “Sau”) in acorns, leaves, hearts and bells. Everyone gets eight cards, usually in two packets of four. Dealing and play go clockwise. Starting to the dealer’s left, everyone says whether they want to play; a solo beats a call game. Anyone who likes their first four cards may knock beforehand, doubling the game.

Call game: The declarer calls an ace in acorns, leaves or bells that they don’t hold, but must hold a card of that suit. Whoever has the called ace is the partner, without revealing it. The called ace must be played the first time its suit is led and can’t be discarded before then. If its holder has at least four cards of that suit, they may lead another card of the suit (“running away”).

Card ranking: In a call game the trumps, high to low, are the Obers, then the Unters, each in the order acorns, leaves, hearts, bells, then ace, 10, king, 9, 8, 7 of hearts. The other suits rank ace, 10, king, 9, 8, 7. In a suit solo the chosen suit takes the place of hearts. In Wenz only the Unters are trumps, in Geier only the Obers; the others rank below the king in their suit. In a suit Wenz the Unters and the chosen suit are trumps. In Bettel there are no trumps (ace, king, Ober, Unter, 10, 9, 8, 7), and the declarer must not take a trick. In a wedding, someone with only one trump offers it; whoever takes it becomes the partner.

Tricks: The player to the dealer’s left leads. You must follow the suit led, and trump if trump is led; if you can’t, you may play any card except the called ace. The highest trump wins, otherwise the highest card of the suit led. The winner of a trick leads the next.

Card points: Ace counts 11, ten 10, king 4, Ober 3, Unter 2 and the rest 0 – 120 in total. The declaring side wins with at least 61 points. A side with 30 points or fewer is schneider, a side without a trick is schwarz. Runners are the top trumps a side holds in an unbroken run, starting with the Ober of acorns (in Wenz with the Unter of acorns).

Game value: A call game is worth the base tariff, usually 1, while solo, Wenz, Geier, suit Wenz and Bettel are worth the solo tariff, usually 5. Add one unit each for schneider and schwarz, and one unit per runner once there are at least three (two in Wenz and Geier). Tout – announcing that a solo will take every trick – doubles the value, as does Bettel with open cards. Every knock, Kontra and Re doubles it again.

Settling up: In a call game and a wedding both winners get the game value and both losers pay it. In a solo the soloist gets or pays the value from each of the other three, so three times.

Ramsch: If nobody wants to play, everyone plays for themselves with the call-game trumps. Whoever has the most card points pays everyone else the base tariff; this doubles for each player without a trick. If someone takes every trick (durchmarsch), they win as in a solo.

End of the game: Play up to the agreed number of rounds or open-ended; the highest total wins.

Goal

As the declaring side, take at least 61 card points – every trick in a tout, none in Bettel. In the end the highest total wins.

Scoring at a glance

The declaring side wins with at least 61 card points. Game value: call game 1; solo, Wenz, Geier, suit Wenz and Bettel 5 (tariff adjustable), plus 1 each for schneider, schwarz and every runner from three on (from two in Wenz and Geier). Tout, every knock, Kontra and Re double it. In a call game everyone gets or pays the value, in a solo the soloist triple.

Keeping score in the app

Bavarian card game for four with 32 cards. In a call game it is two against two, in a solo, Wenz or Geier one against three; whoever takes at least 61 card points wins. You enter the game, who plays, the card points and the runners, and the app settles up by your tariff.
