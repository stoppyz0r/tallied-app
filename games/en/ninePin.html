Ninepins

Nine pins: full racks, clearing and pin games · 1–18 players · Ball, throwing and lane games

What is it?

Ninepin bowling on a lane with nine pins set up in a diamond, with the king in the middle – unlike tenpin bowling with its triangle of ten. It is played as sport bowling with full racks and clearing, plus many pin games for a bowling night such as house number, wreath or Christmas tree, often with a kitty.

How it’s played

Basics: The nine pins stand in a diamond with the king in the middle. Whoever is up throws their whole visit in a row, then the next player. “Full rack” means all nine pins stand before every throw. “Clearing” means fallen pins stay down until all nine have fallen; then the pins are reset. All nine means every pin with one throw, a wreath means all except the king. A gutter ball goes into the gutter, a zero stays on the lane without hitting anything; only the gutter ball counts as a miss.

Sport bowling: First 15 throws at full racks, then 15 throws clearing; all pins are added up. Most pins win, on a tie more pins in clearing, then fewer misses. “Full racks only” works the same without clearing.

House number: Everyone throws three times at full racks; each throw gives a digit – hundreds, tens, ones, in throwing order or chosen freely. Whoever has the highest number (or the lowest in a low round) gets a point; in low rounds a gutter ball counts 9. The most round wins take the game.

Clearing by throw count: Everyone clears until all nine pins are down; whoever needs the fewest throws in total wins. With the points table you have at most eight throws: clearing with one throw scores 40 points, 5 fewer with each further throw.

Picture bonus and wreath: Played at full racks. With picture bonus, all nine, a wreath and your own pictures earn extra points, usually 10. In the wreath game only wreaths count, on a tie the pins.

Wreath duel: Everyone has six clearing throws and tries to leave only the king standing. A wreath scores more the fewer throws it takes: 100 points with one throw, then 80, 70, 60, 50 and 40; then the pins are reset. If the king falls, the visit is over, and each gutter ball costs 30 points.

Christmas tree: Each player or team has a tree of numbers from 1 to 9, such as one 1, two 2s, three 3s and so on. With a full-rack throw you strike the number of pins down from your own tree; if it is already gone there, it is struck from everyone else’s. The game ends as soon as a tree has no 5 left; the most struck numbers win.

Ladder 1 to 9: With one full-rack throw each, you must first knock down exactly one pin, then exactly two and so on up to nine. Whoever makes the 9 is done; the round is played to the end.

Teams and kitty: In many games you can play in teams, in which case the members’ total counts. With a kitty, each gutter ball costs an amount, usually 10 cents, and your own penalties can be added at any time. Throws, rounds and variants are set when you create the game.

Goal

Depending on the game, get the most pins, the best house number, the most wreaths or points, or finish the ladder first.

Scoring at a glance

At full racks all nine pins stand before every throw; when clearing, the pins are only reset once all have fallen. All nine: every pin with one throw; wreath: all except the king. Gutter ball: into the gutter, counts as a miss; zero: no pins, but no miss. Everyone throws their visit in a row; what wins depends on the chosen game.

Keeping score in the app

Ninepin bowling: sport play with full racks and clearing, or pin games such as house number, wreath, wreath duel, Christmas tree and ladder. After each throw you tap the number of pins down or the pins on the diagram. The app knows whose turn it is and what is still standing, counts gutter balls and the kitty, and works out the result.
