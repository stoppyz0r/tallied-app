Castles & Roads

Build a landscape, claim cities and roads · 2–6 players · Board and tile games
Known as Carcassonne

What is it?

A tile-laying game with square landscape tiles and playing figures in five colors. Everyone builds one shared landscape of cities, roads, monasteries and fields and places their own figures on it to score when features are completed. At the end, unfinished features and the farmers in the fields count too.

How it’s played

Setup: The starting tile lies face up in the middle, and the other tiles are shuffled face down. Everyone gets the figures of one color, seven in the base game. Players take turns.

Placing a tile: On your turn, draw a tile and add it to the landscape. Every edge it touches must match: road to road, city to city, field to field. You may rotate it. If a tile fits nowhere, set it aside and draw a new one.

Placing a figure: Then you may place one of your figures on the tile you just placed – as a knight in a city, a thief on a road, a monk in a monastery or a farmer in a field. There must be no figure yet in the whole connected feature, not even your own. If two occupied features later grow together, several figures can end up in them. Farmers lie down; they stay until the end of the game.

Completing features: A city is complete when its wall is closed all around; a road when both ends lead to a crossroads, city or monastery or it forms a loop; a monastery when all eight surrounding spaces have tiles. Completed features score immediately, and their figures return to the supply.

Scoring during play: Whoever has the most figures in a completed city or on a completed road gets the points; on a tie, everyone with the most figures gets the full points. A completed city scores 2 points per tile and per shield, a completed road 1 point per tile, a completed monastery 9 points.

Final scoring: After the last tile, unfinished features that are still occupied score: city 1 point per tile and per shield, road 1 point per tile, monastery 1 point plus 1 per surrounding tile. Each field gives the majority of its farmers 3 points for every completed city bordering it. The highest total wins.

Expansion: With the inns and cathedrals option, the app scores completed roads with an inn at 2 points per tile and completed cities with a cathedral at 3 per tile and shield; unfinished, both score nothing.

Goal

Control cities, roads, monasteries and fields with well-placed figures. After the last tile and final scoring, the highest total wins.

Scoring at a glance

Completed: city 2 per tile and shield, road 1 per tile, monastery 9. Unfinished at the end: city 1 per tile and shield, road 1 per tile, monastery 1 plus 1 per neighboring tile; field 3 per bordering completed city. Whoever has the most figures in a feature scores, on a tie everyone with full points. The highest total wins.

Keeping score in the app

Tile-laying game in which square tiles form a shared landscape of cities, roads, monasteries and fields. When a feature is completed, whoever has the most figures there scores. You record each scoring with feature, points and players involved, and the app helps with the math and adds it up.
