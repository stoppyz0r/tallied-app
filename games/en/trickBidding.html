How Many Tricks?

Predict your tricks, hit them exactly · 3–6 players · Card games
Known as Wizard, Oh Hell

What is it?

A trick-taking game with 60 cards: four suits numbered 1 to 13, plus four top cards and four zero cards. Before each round everyone bids how many tricks they will take, and every round the hand grows by one card. It also works with a standard deck without the special cards.

How it’s played

Setup: Shuffle the 60-card deck. In the first round everyone gets one card, and in each later round one more. The deal passes clockwise. After dealing, turn over the next card of the deck to set the trump suit: cards of that suit beat all ordinary cards of other suits. If it is a top card, the dealer chooses trumps; if it is a zero card or no card is left, there are no trumps.

Bidding: Starting to the dealer’s left, everyone bids how many tricks they will win; 0 is allowed. A trick is one card from each player. Bids stand until the end of the round.

Playing tricks: The player to the dealer’s left leads the first trick. You must follow the first ordinary suit played if you can; otherwise you may play any card. Top and zero cards may always be played. If a trick starts with zero cards, the first ordinary card sets the suit. If a top card comes first or leads the trick, nobody has to follow suit.

Winning a trick: The first top card wins the trick. Without a top card, the highest trump wins, otherwise the highest card of the suit led. Zero cards lose; if only zero cards were played, the first one wins. The winner of a trick leads the next.

Scoring: If you win exactly as many tricks as you bid, you get 20 points plus 10 per trick – a correct bid of 0 counts too. If you miss, you lose 10 points per trick of difference.

End of the game: Play until the whole deck is dealt: 20 rounds with three players, 15 with four, 12 with five and 10 with six. The highest total wins. With a standard deck (52 cards, ace high) the special cards are left out and fewer rounds are possible; you can shorten the number of rounds when setting up the game.

Goal

Take exactly the number of tricks you bid each round. After the last round the highest total wins.

Scoring at a glance

In round 1 everyone gets one card, and one more each round after that. Before play everyone bids their number of tricks. An exact bid earns 20 points plus 10 per trick; each trick off costs 10 points. After the last round (60 cards divided by the number of players) the highest total wins.

Keeping score in the app

Trick-taking game in which each round deals one more card; special top and zero cards make predicting tricky. Only an exact bid earns plus points. You enter the bids and the tricks won, and the app works out the points.
