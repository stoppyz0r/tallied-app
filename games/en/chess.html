Chess

Checkmate, with a chess clock if you like · 2 players · Board and tile games

What is it?

The classic strategy game on 64 squares: white and black move in turn with king, queen, rooks, bishops, knights and pawns.

How it’s played

Setup: Place the board with a light square at the bottom right. Pawns go on the second rank, behind them from the outside in: rooks, knights, bishops, the queen on her color and the king. White starts.

Moving: The king moves one square in any direction, the queen any distance straight or diagonally, the rook straight, the bishop diagonally, the knight two squares straight and one to the side, jumping. Pawns move one square forward, two on their first move, and capture diagonally forward. Moving onto a square with an opposing piece captures it.

Special moves: castling (the king moves two squares toward a rook, which jumps over it), capturing en passant and promoting a pawn on the last rank.

Check and mate: An attacked king is in check and must be freed at once. If that is impossible, it is checkmate and the game is lost.

Draw: by stalemate (no legal move but not in check), by agreement, by threefold repetition or when nobody can mate any more.

Chess clock: With a clock each side has time for the whole game, often with an increment after each move. Whoever runs out of time loses.

Goal

Checkmate the opposing king: it is attacked and cannot escape.

Scoring at a glance

Checkmate wins; a draw by stalemate, agreement or repetition. With a chess clock, whoever runs out of time loses.

Keeping score in the app

The board game for two. After each game you tap the win or the draw – the app keeps the encounter, the ratings and, if you like, a chess clock with time and increment.
