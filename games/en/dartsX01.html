Darts X01

Count down from 501 or 301 to exactly zero · 1–8 players · Darts

What is it?

The classic darts game on a steel-tip or soft-tip board with the numbers 1 to 20, double and triple rings and the bull. Every hit is subtracted from the starting score, usually 501, and whoever reaches exactly zero wins the leg – usually with a double as the last dart. Several legs make a set.

How it’s played

Setup: You need a dartboard, three darts each and a fixed throwing line. Beforehand, agree on the starting score (usually 501, also 101, 301, 701 or 1001), how to start and finish, and how many legs or sets it takes to win. Everyone starts each leg at the starting score.

Board values: The large areas score the number on the edge. The narrow outer ring is the double and scores twice, the narrow inner ring the triple and scores three times. The outer bull scores 25, the bullseye in the middle 50, which counts as a double. A miss or a dart that falls out scores 0. Three darts can score at most 180.

Visits: Players take turns throwing up to three darts; that is a visit. The points are subtracted from your remaining score.

Finishing: With double-out, the usual rule, your last dart must hit a double that brings your score to exactly zero – for example double 16 with 32 left. With straight-out any dart reaching exactly zero will do, with master-out a triple also counts, and with bull-out the last dart must hit the bull. With double-in, points only count from the first double. A successful checkout ends the leg at once.

Bust: If your score drops below zero, leaves exactly 1 with double-out, or reaches zero without a valid last dart, the visit is a bust. It scores nothing, and your score goes back to what it was before the visit.

End of the game: The winner of a leg is credited with it, and the next player throws first in the next leg. Without sets, whoever first wins the agreed number of legs (usually three) wins. With sets, the first to that number of legs wins the set, and the first to the agreed number of sets wins the game.

Goal

Be the first to bring your score down to exactly zero, usually finishing on a double. Whoever first wins the required legs or sets wins.

Scoring at a glance

Everyone starts at the chosen score and throws up to three darts per visit; the points are subtracted. Whoever reaches exactly zero wins the leg – with double-out the last dart must be a double. Below zero, 1 left with double-out, or zero without a valid last dart is a bust: the visit scores nothing. Whoever first wins the required legs or sets wins.

Keeping score in the app

Darts game in which everyone counts down from a starting score such as 501; whoever hits exactly zero wins the leg. You enter the points of each visit or tap dart by dart, and the app works out what’s left, spots busts and checkouts, suggests checkout routes, counts legs and sets and says who’s up.
