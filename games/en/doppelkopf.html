Doppelkopf

The queens of clubs team up · 4–6 players · Card games

What is it?

Doppelkopf uses two of every card from 9 to ace: 48 cards worth 240 points in total. With four players it is usually two against two, and who is on which side only shows during play. In a normal game the tens of hearts, all queens, all jacks and diamonds are trumps.

How it’s played

Setup: The deck has the 9, 10, jack, queen, king and ace in clubs, spades, hearts and diamonds, each card twice. Everyone gets twelve cards; with five players the dealer sits out, with six the player opposite too. Dealing and play go clockwise. Before the first trick, players announce reservations such as a marriage or a solo; otherwise a normal game is played.

Teams: In a normal game the two players holding a queen of clubs form the “Re” team, the other two are “Kontra”. Nobody reveals who belongs together; it shows during play. If you hold both queens of clubs, you can announce a marriage: whoever else wins one of the first three tricks first becomes your partner. If nobody does, you play alone as in a diamonds solo. Without an announcement you play alone against everyone in secret (silent solo).

Trump ranking in a normal game, high to low: ten of hearts, then the queens and then the jacks, each in the order clubs, spades, hearts, diamonds, then ace, 10, king and 9 of diamonds. Clubs, spades and hearts rank ace, 10, king, 9; hearts has no 10 outside the trumps. If two identical cards are in a trick, the one played first wins.

Taking a turn: The player to the dealer’s left leads. A trick is one card from each of the four players. You must follow the suit led, and trump if trump is led; if you can’t, you may play any card. The highest trump wins, otherwise the highest card of the suit led. The winner takes the trick and leads the next one.

Card points: Ace counts 11, ten 10, king 4, queen 3, jack 2 and nine 0. After twelve tricks each team counts its points. Re wins with at least 121, Kontra with at least 120; if only Kontra has announced, 120 is enough for Re.

Announcements: By saying “Re” or “Kontra” you announce that your team will win; you can do so until the second card is led. Building on that, you can declare that the other team will stay below 90, 60 or 30 points or take no trick (“black”), each one card later. Failing a declaration loses the game.

Solos: One player plays alone against the other three. In a suit solo the chosen suit replaces diamonds as trumps; tens of hearts, queens and jacks stay trumps. In a queens solo only queens are trumps, in a jacks solo only jacks, and in a no-trump solo there are none (ace, 10, king, queen, jack, 9). In an announced solo the soloist leads.

Game value: The winning team gets 1 point for winning, 1 point each if the other team stays below 90, 60 or 30 points or takes no trick, 2 points for each announcement of Re or Kontra and 1 point for each declaration. If Kontra wins a normal game, they get 1 point “against the queens”. In a normal game extra points are added whoever wins: a trick worth 40 or more points (doppelkopf), catching the other team’s ace of diamonds (fox) and winning the last trick with the jack of clubs (Charlie).

Scoring and end: The winners gain the value and the others lose it. In a solo the soloist gains or pays three times as much. Play up to the agreed number of rounds or open-ended; the highest total wins. House rules such as bock rounds, poverty, compulsory solos or more extra points can be set when you create the game.

Goal

Take the majority of card points with your team – Re needs 121, Kontra 120 – and make good on your announcements. In the end the highest score wins.

Scoring at a glance

Re wins with 121 and Kontra with 120 of the 240 card points, unless a declaration requires more. Game value: 1 for winning, 1 each for below 90, 60, 30 and black, 2 per announcement, 1 per declaration, plus extra points. The winning team gains the value and the other loses it; in a solo it counts triple for the soloist.

Keeping score in the app

Trick-taking game with 48 cards for four; with five, the dealer sits out, with six the player opposite too. Usually the two holding the queens of clubs play together without the others knowing. You enter the game type, card points, announcements and extra points, and the app works out the score by tournament rules or your house rules.
