Mini Golf

Hole out in as few strokes as possible · 1–8 players · Ball, throwing and lane games

What is it?

Mini golf on a course with holes full of obstacles – ramps, loops, tubes. With a putter and ball you play each hole from the tee to the cup and count your strokes. A hole in one means holing out with the first stroke.

How it’s played

Setup: You need a putter, a ball and the course’s scorecard – the app replaces it. Usually 18 holes are played, with a stroke limit per hole, usually six.

Playing a hole: Place the ball in the tee area. One player finishes the hole, then the next; only when everyone is done do you move on to the next hole. Every stroke counts, even a miss.

Continuing: Play from wherever the ball stops. If it leaves the hole or fails to clear an obstacle, the course rules apply, often restarting from the tee or a marked line. You may not simply move the ball closer to the cup.

Holing out and the limit: Holing out with the first stroke is a hole in one and scores 1. If the ball still isn’t in the cup after the limit, pick it up and write down one stroke more than the limit – 7 with a limit of six. If you hole out with the last stroke allowed, write down the actual number.

Scoring and end: After the last hole all strokes are added up; the lowest total wins. On a tie, whoever has more holes in one is ahead. If you enter the par of each hole, you also see how many strokes you are over or under par; this doesn’t change the standings.

Goal

Play every hole in as few strokes as possible. After the last hole the lowest total wins.

Scoring at a glance

Every stroke counts, holing out with the first is a hole in one. If you haven’t holed out by the limit, one stroke more is recorded, 7 with a limit of six. After the last hole the lowest total wins, on a tie more holes in one. Par only shows the difference to the expected strokes.

Keeping score in the app

Mini golf on 1 to 36 holes: every stroke into the cup counts, and fewer is better. After each hole you tap everyone’s strokes. The app knows the stroke limit and records one extra for a ball that wasn’t holed, shows the difference to par, counts holes in one and works out the totals and standings.
